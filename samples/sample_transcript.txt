Teacher: Good morning everyone. Today we're going to read a short story called The Friendship Tree. Before we begin, who can remind us what we mean by the main idea of a story?
Student: It's what the story is mostly about.
Teacher: Good. And what is one strategy we use to find the main idea?
Student: We look at what keeps happening.
Teacher: Right. We look for what keeps coming up across the story. Okay, I'm going to read the first page out loud. Listen carefully because afterwards I'll ask you to think about what the author wants us to learn from this part.
Teacher: Once upon a time there were two friends named Mia and Theo. They lived next door to each other and every afternoon they would sit under the old oak tree at the corner. One day Mia found out her family was moving to a new city. She did not know how to tell Theo. So she sat under the tree all alone and cried. The next day Theo came to find her. He brought her favorite cookies and said even if you move I will still be your friend forever.
Teacher: Okay. What do you think the main idea of this part might be?
Student: That moving is sad.
Teacher: Hmm. Moving being sad is part of it. But think bigger. What is the author really trying to teach us through what the characters do?
Student: That friends help each other when something hard happens.
Teacher: Yes. How do you know that from the story?
Student: Because Theo brought her cookies and said he would still be her friend even when she moves away.
Teacher: Excellent. You used evidence from the text. That is exactly what good readers do. Now turn to your partner and share one example from your own life when a friend helped you through something hard. You have one minute. Go.
Teacher: Okay friends, bring it back. Who would like to share what their partner said?
Student: My partner said her friend helped her when her dog died.
Student: My friend helped me when I was scared on the first day of school.
Teacher: Those are beautiful examples. Now I want you to think about this. The author could have set this story anywhere. Why do you think the author chose to put the friends under a tree?
Student: Maybe because it's a quiet place.
Student: Maybe the tree is like their friendship because it's big and strong.
Teacher: Wow. Say more about that.
Student: Like the tree has been there a long time and their friendship has been there a long time too.
Teacher: That is beautiful thinking. You connected a symbol in the story to a deeper meaning, and that is what authors do on purpose. So now I want you to imagine you are the author and you have to write a second part to this story. How could you show that Mia and Theo's friendship is still strong even after she moves? Think about that for a moment. You don't have to answer out loud yet.
Teacher: Alright. For the rest of the lesson I want you to write one paragraph in your notebook. In your paragraph, tell me the main idea of this part of the story and one piece of evidence from the text that supports it. You have ten minutes. I will be walking around to help anyone who is stuck. Any questions before we start?
Student: Do we have to write in complete sentences?
